Dragon Age: Origins · proxy d3d9.dll

Two things fragment. They are not the same thing.

lyrium fixes memory exhaustion at two independent levels of the same 32-bit process. Confusing them is what made the fault hard to find, so this is the map: what breaks where, and which component acts on it.

The nesting

The engine asks Windows for one enormous block at startup and then carves everything it needs out of that block itself. Windows sees a single reservation and never looks inside. That boundary is where the two levels divide.

Level 1 — the address space

What fragments it. Managed textures each keep a full CPU-side copy. Every copy over 508 KB becomes its own reservation, and they turn over constantly at varying sizes.

What lyrium does. Creates eligible textures in D3DPOOL_DEFAULT, which has no CPU copy, and hands the engine a pagefile-backed buffer when it locks one. The duplicate is never made.

Reported as. va[…] — below2g, largest_free.

Level 2 — inside the main pool

What fragments it. The engine's own small, long-lived allocations, scattered through the pool like pillars. One session handed back 330 MB without the largest gap growing by a byte.

What lyrium does. Builds a second heap from the engine's own allocator class and routes everything ≥ 1 MB into it.

Reported as. pool[…] and side[…] — largest_free, full.

The fault at level 2

The engine periodically needs 71.6 MB in one unbroken piece. As the pinned blocks accumulate, the largest gap falls past that line — and the engine does not fail or complain. It checks, sees no room, and skips loading the asset.

Nothing is ever attempted, so nothing is ever counted. Through the crash that emptied Denerim market, every failure counter in the process read zero.

The routing rule

lyrium already detours the engine's allocator entry point. When the side pool is armed, it serves large requests directly through the pool's own vtable rather than pushing a thread tag — the engine's push silently no-ops once the tag stack is 32 deep while its pop always decrements, and an unbalanced pair would misroute every later allocation on that thread.

What this does not do

Neither fix prevents fragmentation. Relocation removes the fragmenter at level 1. The side pool leaves level 2 fragmenting exactly as much as before and moves out the only thing that needed contiguity.

The side pool is the one arena that still depends on contiguity, so it inherits the whole problem — but it holds tens of large blocks rather than a million small ones, so a freed block's neighbours are usually free too and they merge. Its clear run has been measured recovering, from 803.9 back to 850.4 MB, which the main pool has never once done.

Nothing here helps with texture address space. Textures are D3D allocations competing for the same 2 GB, and on a stock executable a heavy texture list runs out of room whatever the pools are sized at. That is what the 4 GB patch is for.

Every figure measured on one install and one route. The overlay reports the same three arenas live, so a different setup shows its own trouble rather than inheriting these assumptions.
